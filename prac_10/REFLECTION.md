## Estimates

Regarding the **estimates** that you did for practical tasks...

### How was your estimate accuracy usually?

My estimate accuracy usually was under the amount of time I actually needed to complete my program by about 10 to 20
minutes.

### How did your estimate accuracy improve or change during the course of the subject?

I would look over the task and give myself a bit more time than I would have originally given myself to allow me to have

as efficient as possible and also make sure it is up to industry standards with doc-strings and comments.

## Code Reviews

### What have you learned from being reviewed by other people?

Being reviewed by other people has helped because as you are the one writing all the code, little mistakes can be easily
missed like a missed full-stop or a naming convention could be used inappropriately. This is where the code review
helped as the person doing the review could look at the code with a new perspective and see how the code could be
improved and identify the little naming mistakes.

### What have you learned from doing code reviews of other people?

I have learnt that there are multiple ways to do the same pieces of code. Some ways are better than others. It has also
showed me that it is easy to miss small things as I have also picked up on lots of little things.

### Good Code Review 1

[Could you put the colour input at the end of the while loop instead?](https://github.com/MadiCatanzaro/cp1404practicals/pull/1)

### Explanation

In one of the Peer Reviews I did, I identified that they were duplicating code at the end of each if statement inside
their while loop to prime the loop at the end. I then suggested if they could just put one priming statement at the end
of their while loop, and then they could get rid of the duplicate statements.

### Good Code Review 2

[This name looks a bit like a dictionary name, maybe rename to new_guitar ?](https://github.com/mitchell-royan/cp1404practicals/pull/3)

### Explanation

In this code review I identified a naming convention for a variable looked like a dictionary naming convention with
the 'to' in it. I then gave them a suggestion to what they could call it instead.
